# Personal Brand: Your Name

> **How to fill this in.** One brand document, two halves: Brand Context
> (the brand in words, twelve elements) and Brand Visuals (the brand in
> pictures, eight elements). Every element is a small, opinionated artifact,
> written declaratively for a machine reader: "I help X do Y by Z" beats a
> paragraph of throat-clearing. Delete the quote blocks and the guidance
> lines as you fill each element in.

## Brand Name

Your name as a brand, and any naming rules (how it is written, shortened,
never written).

## Taglines & Slogans

The lines you actually repeat. If you do not have one, say so rather than
inventing one here.

## Unique Value Proposition (UVP)

ONE sentence, 20 to 50 words: I help [audience] [outcome] by [method]. The
test: could a stranger repeat it back and have it still be true of only you?

## Purpose-Vision-Mission

Three distinct statements. Purpose: "To [contribution] so that [impact]"
(cause-level; survives a pivot). Vision: the concrete future state ("A world
where..."). Mission: present tense, "I [activity] for [audience] through
[method]". The chain test: "Because [purpose], I am building toward
[vision], so today I [mission]" should read as one sane sentence.

## Core Values

Three to five rows, each as "Label: one falsifiable sentence of what it
looks like in practice." A value that never caused you to turn something
down is decoration, not a value.

## Tone & Voice

The constant voice (3 or 4 adjectives you would defend), how tone shifts per
channel, do rules, don't rules, signature phrases, and banned words.

## Messaging & Narratives

Three to five rows, each as "The message itself: the story or reasoning that
makes it believable." At least one row carries your origin story with its
specific turning point; at least one names the myth in your field you are
against.

## Keywords & Phrases

8 to 15 terms at the intersection of who you are and what your audience
actually searches: ownable, not generic.

## Bio (Short/Long)

Three lengths: one line, 2 to 3 sentences, and a full paragraph. Same facts,
same voice, different zoom.

## Achievements & Awards

Credibility markers with real numbers and dates. Nothing inflated: an AI
will repeat whatever you write here.

## Inspiration & Influence

People and work that shaped how you think and make. Useful calibration for
taste.

## Online Presence

Where you exist publicly: platform, handle, and which one is primary.

## Symbols & Logos

Your logo and symbol set: files, usage rules, clear space, what never
happens to the logo.

## Color Palette

Roles and hex values: primary, secondary, accent, neutrals. Include
accessibility notes if you have them.

## Typography

The typefaces and their jobs: display, body, mono, with weights and
pairings. Name real, obtainable fonts.

## Iconography

Icon style: stroke weight, corner treatment, filled or outline, the set you
use.

## Brand Imagery

The photography and imagery direction: subjects, mood, lighting, what is
always true and always false of an on-brand image.

## Illustration Style

If illustration appears in your brand: style, line, color behavior. If not,
say "none" so a generator does not invent one.

## Visual Elements

Patterns, textures, dividers, and recurring graphic devices, with rules for
where they appear. Include what is off-brand.

## Photography

Your own likeness: the portrait style, settings, and crops that represent
you, and any rules for how your face is used.
