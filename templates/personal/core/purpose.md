# Purpose & Meaning

> **How to fill this in.** This section codifies *why you do what you do* using
> [Ikigai 2.0](https://sloww.co/ikigai-2-0), Kyle Kowalski's corrected model of ikigai.
> Work the sections in order: what you love first (it fuels everything else), then
> natural talents, then what the world needs. Money comes deliberately last — a
> byproduct of purpose, not a requirement of it. Answer honestly and specifically;
> vague purpose statements produce vague AI output. Delete the guidance blocks as you
> go.

## What gets me up in the morning

The thing that energizes you, independent of money or recognition — what you'd keep
doing if no one paid or noticed. Be concrete.

## What I find meaningful

What matters to you — the values and contributions that make effort feel worthwhile,
even when it's hard. Small everyday sources of meaning count as much as grand ones.

## What I'm naturally built for

Talents and gifts, not hard-won skills. Skills are things you trained; talents are the
things that always came easier to you than to most people. Your personality profile is
a clue here. If you love something but have no gift for it, that's a hobby — worth
keeping, but it doesn't belong in this section.

## What the world needs from me

Something you serve that's larger than yourself — a family, a community, a craft, a
global problem. Any size counts. Loving something and being gifted at it can curdle
into ego without this check.

## How I make a living from this

Money as a byproduct of the above, not the aim. The practical test: what does the
world *need* that people also *want* enough to pay for? How does that translate (or
how will it translate) into work and income?

## My purpose, in one line

Synthesize the above into a single sentence. This is the line an AI can reason from.
Example: *"I help people who feel unseen turn what makes them different into how they're
known."* You may have more than one ikigai — if so, lead with the one you most want AI
to reflect.

## Direction (next 1–3 years)

Where this purpose is taking you right now. Purpose is a process, not a destination —
what are you building toward in the near term?
