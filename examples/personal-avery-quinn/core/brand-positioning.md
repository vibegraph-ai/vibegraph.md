# Brand — Positioning

*Fictional example.*

## Positioning statement

I help solo founders and independent experts sound like themselves in public, so their writing builds trust and demand instead of blending into the feed.

## Unique value proposition

Most brand help is either strategy with no words to show for it, or copywriting with no strategy underneath. I do both in one engagement: the positioning *and* the voice, so what we decide is immediately usable everywhere you write.

## Purpose, vision, mission

- **Purpose** — help people who feel invisible turn what makes them different into how they're known.
- **Vision** — a web where the most trusted voice in a niche is the clearest one, not the loudest.
- **Mission** — run positioning intensives and voice work that leave clients able to write as themselves, with or without me.

## Core values

- **Specificity over polish.** A sharp, slightly rough sentence beats a smooth, generic one. Always.
- **Honesty over flattery.** I tell clients what isn't working. That's what they're paying for.
- **Craft is respect.** Sloppy work tells the client you didn't care. I care.
- **Clarity is kindness.** Confusing your audience is a form of disrespect. Don't.

## Tone & voice

- **Voice (constant):** warm, direct, wry. Generous with credit, allergic to hype. Plain-spoken but not plain.
- **Tone (varies):** more playful in a social post, more measured in a client proposal, but never stiff and never salesy.
- **Do:** short sentences; concrete examples; second person; the occasional dry aside; strong verbs.
- **Don't:** hedging ("perhaps," "it could be argued"), hype ("game-changing," "revolutionary"), exclamation points, corporate abstractions, three-adjective pile-ups, em-dash overuse.
- **Signature phrases I actually use:** "sound like yourself," "specific beats clever," "say the true thing plainly."
- **Words I never use:** "leverage" (as a verb), "unlock," "elevate," "synergy," "thought leader," "guru."

## Messaging & narratives

- **Origin story:** spent a decade ghostwriting for executives who all started to sound identical, realized the problem was never the writing — it was that nobody had done the positioning first.
- **Point of view:** personal branding has become a costume everyone wears. The winners take the costume off. Differentiation isn't louder; it's truer.
- **Known for:** the belief that most "personal brand" advice makes people more generic, not less.
